# Why I Built Jobiest

**Draft for Philip to review and paste into LinkedIn. Not published.**

The job search asks people to be confident at exactly the moment many feel least certain.

You find a role that might fit. Then comes the work: understand the job description, decide which experience matters, adjust your CV, write a cover letter, track the application, and prepare for an interview that may or may not happen.

I started building Jobiest because I wanted that process to feel more manageable without taking the candidate out of control.

A job application is personal. It represents someone's experience, time, and hopes. I did not want to build a product that treated applications like a volume game or quietly sent them on a person's behalf. Jobiest is designed around a simple principle: prepare carefully, show your work, and let the person decide what gets submitted.

That principle shapes the product. Jobiest helps people keep their career information together, prepare and tailor resumes and CVs, work with job descriptions, and organize applications. We have also built free career tools, including an ATS resume scanner, skills matcher, interview question generator, and salary insights tool. The point is to make useful help accessible, while being clear about what a tool can and cannot tell you.

We are also building a verified job discovery experience. A listing should point people back to its source, and candidates should check the employer's requirements and eligibility before applying. A job platform has a responsibility to be careful about what it calls verified and what it promises.

Building this from Nigeria has kept the problem close to home. People are looking for local opportunities, remote work, and a fair way to present their skills. Good candidates should not have to spend every evening rebuilding the same documents from scratch, and they should never be pushed to claim experience they do not have.

Jobiest is still a work in progress. I am learning from every product decision, every bug, and every conversation with someone navigating a job search. The goal is not to promise an interview or a job. The goal is to help people do the work with more clarity, less repetition, and control over every application.

If you are building a career, hiring, or working on tools that make opportunity more accessible, I would be glad to hear what you think. What part of the job search do you wish someone had made easier?
